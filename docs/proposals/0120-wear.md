# Proposal 0120: wear in the real levels

Status: accepted and implemented, 2026-11-06, the sprint through the list.

## Research

The engine could do layers, masks, paint, decals and hex tiling, and the real levels used almost none of it. Of 128 materials 2 had layers and 1 had hex tiling that a level used, and Vesper Deep had no paint or decals, so nearly every surface was one repeating picture. The human pointed at this directly: "We mostly didnt excersize our 8 layer/alpha mask ect stuff in these maps."

Three things stood in the way.

- **Nobody can place it by hand.** Vesper Deep has about 24,000 faces. Wear in a real place follows where surfaces meet: damp at the foot of walls, stains from the top, dirt in the open. That's a rule, so a tool can follow it from the level's real shape.
- **Paint edges are soft.** Paint is held at light sample spacing, 32 units in Vesper Deep. With no height map, a layer joined along a flat 0.5, so it faded in as a smooth blur.
- **Hex tiling was half done.** It moved the colour but not the normal map, so bumps didn't match the picture they belonged to.

An image mask can't do this job. It repeats with its texture, so a blob of moss comes back every 232 units, which is the very repeat the human is seeing. Image masks suit a material with structure of its own, and `mossy_stone` keeps one. Paint belongs to the surface and doesn't repeat, so it's what's used here.

Hex tiling garbles anything with courses or a grid, such as blocks, bricks, flags and coffers. It only goes on what has no pattern to break: rock and damp walls.

## Design

- **`wear`**, a new operation, reads the compiled level and puts down ordinary `paint` and `decal` blocks.
  - Strokes run along the foot of walls with gaps left in the line, rise in the middle, and come in patches of damp or dirt about 640 units across. Fewer, thinner strokes come down from the top of walls.
  - Dabs of dirt go on open floors, some with a grime decal.
  - Stains and cracks go on walls, about one for each 420 by 420 units, up to 800 for a level, kept evenly across it.
  - Metal, wood, glass and liquids stay clean.
  - Only surfaces someone can see count. A flood through open space from the level's entities finds them, as the outside of a level is real faces too.
  - It's the same each time for a seed. Running it again replaces only what it made, and undo takes it back.
- **Materials.** All 36 floor, wall, rock and ceiling materials in the six sets take two painted layers: layer 0 is the set's damp wall, and layer 1 its rock, or its flags on rock itself. Rock and damp walls take hex tiling.
- **Paint joins along the picture.** With no height map the join uses the picture's own light and dark, so what's painted reaches dark cracks first, and its edge has the picture's detail at texel scale. The samples stay as they were.
- **Hex tiling moves the normal map too.**
- **Decal art.** Three pictures, `decal_grime`, `decal_stain` and `decal_cracks`, made from noise, with a written spec in the [texture standard](../texture-standard.md).
- **`decal = true`** in a material says it only lies on solid surfaces, so it needs no place among see-through things.

The bake looked at every stroke for every sample, which is 5,500 strokes by 1.5 million samples. Strokes are now filed in a grid of 128 unit cells.

## Proposal

- **Selected approach:** the above.
- **Tradeoffs:** wear is one rule for all levels. It doesn't know a dry tomb from a sewer, beyond `amount` and `seed`, and an author can delete or add to what it makes. The added layers cost every surface using those materials, painted or not.
- **Acceptance criteria:** the real levels show wear that follows their shape, with no pattern and no hard blur, and frame time barely moves.
- **Future extension:** rules for wear in a level's own settings, a button for it in the editor, and more decal art once there's a reason for it.

## Review

Vesper Deep took 5,557 strokes and 800 decals, and Ember Keep 1,429 and 274. `wear` takes 2.0 s and 0.6 s. Vesper Deep's bake went from 21.8 s to 23.8 s, so the grid works.

Frame time in Vesper Deep at 2560 by 1400 on a GTX 1070, averaged from the same view:

| What | Frame time |
|---|---|
| Before, with no layers or hex tiling | 3.98 ms |
| With layers and hex tiling | 4.33 ms |
| With wear, each decal face its own draw | 5.05 ms |
| With wear, decals drawn at once | 4.33 ms |

The third row was the find. Every see-through face was its own draw, sorted far to near, and 800 decals clipped to the surfaces under them make about 4,600 faces. `decal = true` puts them in one draw for each material, ahead of other see-through things, and their cost went below what can be measured.

Faults found by looking and by tests, and fixed: faces worked out as facing inwards, which put dirt on ceilings; wear going on the outside of levels, which used up the decals; an `amount` of 0 still making a stroke on a roll of exactly 0; and truncating decals by order, which would have left the end of a level bare.

The layer limit is 3 for each material and 8 images, not 8 layers. The human's "8 layer" is those 8 image slots. These materials use 5 of them.

Tests fail with their fixes out: the facing test with the sign turned, and the outside faces test with the flood removed.
